# S03-B01: Getting Started with AWS (Regions, Availability Zones, Global vs. Regional)

Udemy (Stéphane Maarek), Section 3. Exam link: Task 1.1 and Task 2.2 (global infrastructure, foundations). Reviewed 2026-10-21.

## 1. The big idea

Where your application runs controls speed for your users, legal compliance, cost, which services you can use, and how well you survive a failure. Regions and Availability Zones are how you control all five.

## 2. Key terms

- **Region:** a cluster of data centers in one geographic area, named like us-east-1 (N. Virginia) or eu-west-3 (Paris). Most services are scoped to a Region.
- **Availability Zone (AZ):** one or more data centers inside a Region with redundant power, networking and connectivity. A Region has 3 to 6 AZs, usually 3. AZs are isolated from each other, so a disaster in one is designed not to cascade, and they are linked by very fast, low-latency networks.
- **Edge location (point of presence):** a site outside the Regions used to deliver content to users with the lowest latency. About 400+ in roughly 90 cities. Covered in detail later in the course.
- **Global service:** no Region to pick. The console shows "Global" and the view is the same everywhere.
- **Regional service:** your resources live in one Region and only appear when that Region is selected.

## 3. How to choose a Region

Four factors:
- **Compliance:** the law may require data to stay in a country. This is a hard limit.
- **Latency:** put the app close to its users.
- **Service availability:** not every Region offers every service, and new services launch in limited Regions first.
- **Pricing:** it varies by Region. Compare it only among Regions that already pass the first three.

There is no fixed ranking beyond that. The scenario's requirements decide.

Example: users in France and French law keeps their data in France, so choose Paris (eu-west-3). Confirm the services you need are offered there. Then spread across its AZs.

## 4. Region vs. AZ vs. edge location

- **Region:** where you run. A factor when choosing (compliance, latency, services, price).
- **AZ:** how you survive a data center failure. You spread your app across them yourself. They are not automatic backups.
- **Edge location:** how content reaches users fast. It is NOT a factor in choosing a Region.

## 5. Global vs. regional services

- **Global:** IAM (Identity and Access Management), Route 53, CloudFront, WAF (Web Application Firewall).
- **Regional:** EC2, Elastic Beanstalk, Lambda, Rekognition. VPCs are regional too (confirmed in my own console).
- **What it means in practice:** a regional resource is invisible when you select a different Region. My VPCs disappeared in Ohio and came back in N. Virginia.
- **Why not everything is global:** servers and networks are physical and must live somewhere. Data location is a legal and design choice. Separate Regions limit how far a problem can spread. Latency and price depend on place. The services that are global are the ones that are naturally the same everywhere: who you are and what you may do (IAM), worldwide DNS (Route 53), and edge services.

## 6. Using the console

- The Region selector is in the top right. It stays on the selected Region as you move between pages. The Home icon does not reset it.
- A service's console shows "Global" or a Region name in the same corner.
- Open services from the Services menu or the search bar.
- The AWS Regional Services list shows which services each Region offers.
- Stay in one Region while learning. Set a default Region (gear icon, user settings). With IAM Identity Center sign-in, the last-visited Region is not remembered.
- Supplemental: the AWS CLI uses its own configured Region, separate from the console's selector.

## 7. Decision rules (requirements lead to choices)

- A law says data stays in a country: choose a Region in that country, whatever the price.
- Users around the world see slow content: use the edge network to deliver content closer to them. Keep choosing the Region by the four factors.
- The app must survive a data center failure in the same Region: spread across two or more AZs.
- A service is missing in your Region: check the Regional Services list, and switch Regions only if latency and compliance allow it.
- A resource seems to have vanished: check the Region selector first.

## 8. Mistakes to watch for (from my own misses)

- Thinking edge locations help decide which Region to choose. They don't.
- Swapping IAM (global) and Lambda (regional).
- Treating AZs as automatic backups. You deploy across them.
- Blending two separate ideas: scope (global or regional) and availability (which Region offers a service).
